Lending protocol internals

Aave's pooled model against Compound's Comet, and why each liquidates differently.

  health = Σ w · amount · price
  maintenance < 0 ──▶ liquidate
  ─────────────────────────────
  Aave: pooled reserves
  Comet: one base asset
Covers
Aave v3 · Compound v3 (Comet)
Angle
Risk isolation as an architectural choice
Question
What happens when the liquidator does not show up?

01 — The shared skeleton

Both protocols are the same idea: deposit collateral, borrow against it, stay above a health threshold or be liquidated. Rates move algorithmically with utilisation, and an oracle decides what the collateral is worth. Where they differ is in how risk is partitioned — and that single decision propagates into everything else.

02 — Aave — pooled reserves

Every listed asset is a reserve in a shared pool; collateral can be lent out, and an account's health factor aggregates weighted collateral against weighted debt. Depositors earn on everything they supply.

Risk is shared, so a bad listing is a pool-wide problem — which is why v3 added isolation mode with per-asset debt ceilings and eMode for correlated pairs. The features are patches over the pooled model's central weakness.

03 — Compound v3 — one borrowable asset per market

Comet inverts it: each market has a single borrowable base asset, and collateral is never lent out. Collateral therefore earns no yield — a real cost paid for a real benefit, which is that a collateral asset misbehaving cannot drain lenders of an unrelated asset.

Liquidation is two-step: the protocol absorbs an underwater position into its own balance sheet first, and only later sells the collateral. That removes the requirement for a well-capitalised liquidator to be present at exactly the wrong moment — the protocol becomes the buyer of last resort, funded by reserves.

04 — What to take from it

The interesting question in a lending protocol is not the interest-rate curve, it is who eats the loss when liquidation fails. Aave answers with liquidator incentives and parameter tuning; Comet answers with reserves and a two-step absorb. Both answers are defensible, and both tell you exactly where the protocol expects to break.
