Keys · Custody

Automated signing behind a policy engine

Separating the ability to sign from the authority to sign.

The key was never the weak point

01 — The problem

A trading bot that signs without a human in the loop holds a hot key. That key carries the authority of everything it can reach — which, without constraints, is the whole treasury.

Most custody discussion is about protecting the key: HSMs, MPC, encrypted storage. All useful, and all answering a different question. Protecting the key is not the same problem as constraining the decision to sign. The 2025 Bybit incident is the clean illustration: no key was broken; a signer was shown one transaction and asked to sign another.

02 — Approach

The signer became the narrowest component in the system, and everything interesting moved in front of it.

A policy layer sits between intent and signature and evaluates every request against explicit rules: per-action value limits, destination allowlists, rate caps per window, and which contract methods are callable at all. Rules are data, versioned and reviewable, not conditionals scattered through business logic.

Anything outside policy escalates to a human rather than failing open. That direction matters: the default on an unrecognised request is refusal, not best-effort execution.

Every decision — allowed, denied, escalated — is written to an append-only log before the signature happens, so the audit trail exists even if the transaction never lands.

03 — Trade-offs

More moving parts, and latency added to every transaction on the path. For a latency-sensitive strategy that cost is real and has to be measured, not assumed away.

Policy also has an operational cost nobody mentions up front: rules drift out of date, and an over-tight rule that blocks legitimate flow at 3am is its own kind of outage. The escalation path has to be staffed to be meaningful.

Bought in exchange: a bounded blast radius. A compromised process can do only what policy already permits, and the ceiling is a number someone chose deliberately.
